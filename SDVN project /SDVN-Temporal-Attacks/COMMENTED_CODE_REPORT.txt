SECTION 5 — WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY KEPT (NOT COMMENTED OUT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following functions were NOT disabled, even though they may appear related
to routing. Here is the reason each was kept:

  5A. centralized_dsrc_data_broadcast()  (lines ~124867 – ~124945)
      STATUS: ACTIVE — kept and enhanced
      REASON: This is the BEACON EXCHANGE function, not routing. It broadcasts
              each vehicle's position, velocity, acceleration, and neighbour
              list to all nearby vehicles on all 7 DSRC channels. This is the
              fundamental V2V awareness mechanism required for:
                - Neighbour discovery (topology awareness)
                - PEM baseline (normal beacon pattern establishes the baseline
                  from which attacks deviate)
                - Per-channel PDR computation (ch_expected_rx tracking)
              DSRC beacons ≠ routing. Beacons say "I am here at position X".
              Routing would say "forward this packet toward destination Y".
              This function does the former only.

  5B. calculate_average_packet_delivery_ratio_routing()  (lines ~120112 – ~120145)
      STATUS: ACTIVE — refactored for per-channel PDR
      REASON: PDR (Packet Delivery Ratio) is one of the five PEM metrics
              required by the project (alongside MCC, AUROC, Tdet, Te2e).
              The function was refactored to compute PDR from beacon delivery
              (ch_expected_rx vs channel_rx_end_count) rather than from routing
              flow delivery. This gives a meaningful PDR even with no routing.

  5C. PemWriteRunSummaryCsv(), PemWriteEventLogCsv()
      STATUS: ACTIVE
      REASON: PEM metric recording is the core of this project. All 12 attack
              scenarios feed into the PEM framework. These write the output
              CSV files used for the FYP report.

  5D. TTW_InitLog(), TTW_SendHelloBeacon(), TTW_SendTopologyUpdate(),
      TTW_StorePacket(), TTW_ReplayAttack(), TTW_RunReplayDetection()
      STATUS: ACTIVE
      REASON: These implement the TTW-S1 attack (attack_scenario=4), which is
              fully implemented and the first of 12 attack variants.

  5E. AttackGetDSRCDevice(), AttackGetControllerIP(), AttackSendRSUToController(),
      AttackSendDSRCBeacon(), AttackSendHeartbeat()
      STATUS: ACTIVE
      REASON: These are attack infrastructure helpers used by all 12 attack
              scenario implementations. They send actual 802.11p frames and
              CSMA packets to simulate the attack channel without routing.

  5F. RSU_dataunicast_agent()  (lines ~132992+)
      STATUS: ACTIVE (scheduled when N_RSUs > 0)
      REASON: RSUs aggregate beacon data from vehicles and forward topology
              snapshots to the controller. This is not routing — it is the
              V2R→R2C topology sensing path that the attack scenarios use as
              their ground truth. Needed for RSU-attacker variants (S2, S4).

  5G. WriteChannelAnalysisCsv()
      STATUS: ACTIVE — called at simulation end
      REASON: Writes per-channel PDR data to
              CHANNEL_DELIVERY_ANALYSIS/<scenario>.csv for report analysis.

  5H. SimpleUdpApplication and CSMA wired network
      STATUS: ACTIVE
      REASON: CSMA Ethernet is the R2C (RSU→Controller) link. It carries
              attack packets (from malicious RSU in S2/S4 variants) to the
              controller. It is not routing — it is the attack delivery path.
